The signal fed in. Steady across the Wi-Fi band (2.40–2.48 GHz); far outside it the rods swap roles and the beam breaks up.

Shorter rods in front. Each one narrows the beam and adds gain, with diminishing returns.

The rod behind. Longer than the driven rod (55.6 mm) it works as a mirror; shorter, the back lobe returns.

Each later director is 1.8 mm shorter. Shorter than the driven rod they act as a lens; longer, they turn into mirrors.

Stretches or squeezes every gap between rods. Tighter gaps raise front-to-back but spoil the feed match.
